# Prior art

What already holds or publishes US public alert messages, checked 2026-11-15. The search
covered Hugging Face (datasets, models and Spaces), Kaggle, Zenodo, Harvard Dataverse and
GitHub for "IPAWS", "Wireless Emergency Alerts" and "emergency alert", plus the resources named
below. Only cards, metadata and listings were read; no dataset file was downloaded. Rows marked
✓ were checked twice, on the resource's own API.

## Is WarnText still new?

Yes. No resource found holds every message in the IPAWS archive, one row per message, with its
sender, time, place, channel and full text, with updates and cancellations linked to the alert
they follow, released in versions that can be cited and updated on a schedule. OpenFEMA is the
living source, but it is nested, unlinked and unversioned, and it cannot be cited at a fixed
state. The closest neighbor is VertexAIco's frozen 60k set, which uses the same OpenFEMA
records but was built to train text generators. It removes duplicates and short and long texts,
leaves out sender, area and channel, flags follow-ups without linking them, and samples rather
than covers the archive. The warning studies that coded messages by hand (Olson et al. 2024;
Kuligowski et al. 2023; Cain et al. 2024) did not share their corpora. What is new, then, is not
that the text can be extracted (it has been, once) but completeness, linked threads, the fields
a content analysis needs on every row, a stated policy for alerts about people, and living,
citable versions.
